## Division and decomposition

I have spent a good time a thinking about death, my death in particular. How will it come? Will it be peaceful and silent or violent and have me screaming off into the silence&#x2026; These thoughts come as I grow older and see how quick life can be snatched from you, how it can take the wrong trajectory and have you knowing that it is coming like it did for the late Hon. Ssegirinya.
A good chunk of my early to mid twenties was spent immersing myself in the stratification and political quagmire I found myself in Uganda. I went through a journey of self-reflection and discovery. As a Ugandan who was barely in touch with my roots and the various intersections of my life, I thought it prudent to absolve myself of my self-ignorance. At least make sense of the current predicament our **cowed** :) society finds itself in. Living in fear of kidnap, murder and economic subterfuge. Broken, alone and alienated Ugandan people and systems only make sense when you&rsquo;re living in there.
In Uganda a country composed of so many faiths and ethnicities, the Abrahamic religions were used as pretext for colonialism that ensued, they divided kingdoms, Catholic against Protestant, with both allying against the Muslims.The maxim gun to enlighten the stubborn ones who did not understand their changing moments.

## Composition

The old kingdoms and empires were almost wiped out from all the manouvering that was happening. Old enemies had to become allies if one were to survive but it was far too late for Ssekabaka Mwanga II and his peer Omukama Kabalega. By the time the dust settled Bunyoro had been decimated and stripped of contentious territories by the converts and  &ldquo;readers&rdquo; of the new religion.
From these ashes rose Uganda, a patchwork nation. A nation that negotiated its independence and still had a patch work of leadership. A king(Kabaka Muteesa II) as president and a socialist radical as president(Milton Obote).
This cocktail was a recipe for chaos leading to a coup executed by Idi Amin(The famous last king of Scotland) and ordered by Obote in 1966, A state of emergency was put on Buganda  and a suspension of the independence agreement/constitution was enacted. From there on the hits just kept coming.
African societies are now in a constant state of flummox confused how to deal with the history that composed them. Each of the individual identities in the patchwork nations caught in a deadlock taking turns to torment each other.

## Tradition.

The only truth I know is that I am&#x2026;Individuality is en vogue and everyone is encouraged to be creative and have independent thoughts (as if there ever is such a thing).
How is it possible to actually have independent thoughts? A mind that has lived is a mind that is polluted with thoughts that are not its own.
It&rsquo;s quiet easy to believe that you can easily control your thoughts, if that were true marketing would not work and illusions would not exist. Given the turbulence of the mind humans need their ideas to be stable.Ideas that are true and will always be true. Tradition and oracles of gods had long filled this gap providing coherence and clearly science is changing that. 
Tradition at least in most societies was everything: religion, custom, laws all wrapped up into one little cocktail. Now tradition is in question, and the average human that could barely follow tradition now has to content with science too. Symbolism is decomposing and as a Ugandan I am left dislocated. Previously well known patterns and norms no longer hold, maybe this is the thesis African tyrants hold, that they will grow their economies by their sheer magnanimity. Though the opposite is true as well, most African leaderships do not believe they will decompose. That the networks of corruption that hold them in power are not transitional and falter when central kleptokrat is fully decomposed.
For any system to last it must be able to recover from failure. This is why I fault the late Qadaffi as the system he had built crambled around him, so did the nation.
I await to see how Ugandans will deal with the systematic failure that will inevitably follow.
